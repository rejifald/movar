Stop the survivor tooltip seeding a dropdown language list with hover traps. On bigfive-test.com, opening the language switcher and reading down it meant dodging eight popups that opened over the very options you were trying to reach.

`annotateSurvivingLinks` attaches a tooltip to **every** surviving classified entry in a picker. That was written for the shape every picker fixture in the corpus has — a header strip of two or three entries on one line — where a hidden entry leaves no slot to mark (the four cleanup passes close the gap completely), so a surface anchored to what survived is the only one available, and two or three quiet hover targets in page chrome cost nothing.

bigfive-test.com is the other shape. Its switcher is a HeroUI/react-aria `<Select>` that portals a `<ul role="listbox">` of 42 `<li role="option">` rows into a body-level popover. Nine of those rows carry languages Movar classifies; hiding `ru` leaves eight survivors, so eight of the rows in an open dropdown each grew a tooltip that opens on a 200 ms hover — at `z-index: 2147483646`, above the site's own popover at `100000`, placed `top`, i.e. directly over the rows above the cursor. Sweeping the pointer down a list is exactly how you read one, so every recognised language was a trap, and the tooltips covered the options either side of it. The user's report was the literal consequence: you could not check the other languages.

The fix is to pick the surface from the picker's shape rather than have one surface for all shapes. `@movar/lang-pickers` now reports a `Picker.layout` — `'list'` for an explicit ARIA listbox/menu (`role="listbox"`/`role="menu"`, or rows carrying `role="option"`/`role="menuitem"`), `'inline'` for everything else. `filterPickers` routes on it: a list gets a replace-mode chip curtain standing **in the hidden row's own slot** (`markHiddenEntries`) and no tooltips at all; an inline strip keeps the survivor tooltip exactly as before. A list has somewhere to put the mark — the row the language used to occupy — which is both less intrusive and more informative than a popup hung off a neighbour, and it is the same in-place treatment a concealed content card already gets.

**The chip says `Movar: hidden`, not the language's name.** The row it stands in is surrounded by language names, so an endonym there reads as one more option to pick — and clicking it restores the entry rather than switching to it. The endonym goes to the chip's description instead, which the host carries as its `title` and its aria-label, so the "which language?" answer is one hover or one screen-reader stop away.

The curtain takes **the whole row, at the row's own size** — a new opt-in `block` option on replace-mode curtains, plus a measured `minHeight`. A content-sized mark in a list of full-width rows reads as a small note dropped into the dropdown and leaves the rest of the line looking like dead space; a band reads as a curtained row, and gives the restore click the whole row instead of a few characters. It stays opt-in because the picker-CONTAINER chip lives in a header strip, where a full-width band would blow the strip apart.

Height needs the measurement because replace mode takes the hidden row's box away with it: left to its own content the curtain came out at roughly half its neighbours (17px against 28px in the fixture), which breaks the list's rhythm — a curtained row should be the same row, not a shorter one. `slotHeightFor` reads a still-visible sibling rather than the entry itself, since by the time a chip goes up `filterPickerLinks` has already hidden the entry; un-hiding it to measure would mean a write, a forced reflow and a re-hide on every observer re-fire, and a list picker's rows are uniform by construction anyway. With no measurable sibling it passes 0 and the curtain sizes to its content, exactly as before. The band also drops the chip's `0.8em` font shrink — that floor exists to keep a chip legible when squeezed into tiny header chrome, and here the chip owns a whole row.

**A cover curtain was the other candidate, and on a picker option it is not merely uglier — it leaves the blocked option live.** Measured on the real fixture in Chromium rather than reasoned about:

- `applyCoverSideEffects` contains `target.children` — ELEMENT children. A listbox row's label is overwhelmingly a bare text node (`<li role="option">Russian</li>`) with no element child at all, so nothing is blurred, nothing is marked `aria-hidden`, and nothing is made `inert`. Measured on the fixture, the covered row reports `display: block`, no `aria-hidden`, no `inert` — still a named, live `option` in the listbox. A screen reader still announces it; a keyboard collection (react-aria's included) still holds it; arrowing onto it and pressing Enter still switches the site to Russian. `pointer-events: none` stops the mouse and nothing else. The replace path reports `display: none` and is gone from both trees.
- Visually it fails too: "Russian" stays fully legible under the overlay, reading as a _disabled_ option rather than a removed one, and the pill's size-container collapse in a ~28px row strips it past the description and past the action down to a bare eye mark — no label, no visible way back.

Movar's job in a picker is to take the option away, not to veil it, so the row has to actually go. Making cover mode fit would mean teaching it to contain text nodes — a change inside every cover curtain on every site, for a surface that needs removal rather than concealment.

**A native `<select>` now has its own layout, and a badge beside the control.** Testing the other picker shapes turned up the inverse failure: on a `<select>`, curtain mode hid the blocked `<option>` with no visible trace AND mounted a survivor tooltip on each remaining `<option>` — measured on the `picker-select-ru` fixture, two tooltip hosts anchored to elements that report a 0×0 box even with the control on screen, so they could never be hovered or focused and never opened. The browser draws a `<select>`'s popup outside the document; an `<option>` may not contain an element; its entries can carry nothing at all.

Anchoring the tooltip on the `<select>` itself works but says nothing until someone happens to hover the control, which on a language switcher is a coin flip. So `attachCurtain` gains a third mode, `badge`: a mark placed beside the target, which is not touched at all.

**The badge is built to be incapable of getting in the way**, which a first pass measurably was not — audited in Chromium, it reported `pointer-events: auto` with a hit-test at its own centre landing on it (a click sink beside the control), and it had become `select.nextElementSibling` (breaking `select + X` rules, `:last-child`, `nth-child` and flex gap counts). Now: the host is appended to `document.body`, so the site's tree gains no sibling and its layout does not shift; `pointer-events: none` means it cannot take a click even where it visually overlaps — which is also what makes tracking it by position safe, since drift can be cosmetic but never functional; and it is `aria-hidden` with no `tabindex`, so it adds no tab stop and no second announcement.

Everything interactive hangs off the CONTROL instead. That is not only safer, it is the only thing that works: anchoring the tooltip on the badge passed a unit test and was unreachable in a real browser, because a `<div>` host takes no focus — tabbing through the page went straight past it and opened nothing. The control is already in the tab order, so hovering or focusing it opens the tooltip (hidden languages + "Show hidden options") and expands the badge from the bare sigil to `Movar: hidden` via `data-expanded` (max-width, so the label stays in the accessible name while clipped).

**Re-annotation is now idempotent, which fixes a surface that vanished while you read it.** Every MutationObserver re-fire used to detach each anchor's tooltip and attach an identical replacement. That is invisible for a mark that is always on screen and destructive for one that is not: the replacement starts closed, the pointer is already inside the anchor, so no fresh `mouseenter` ever fires. Measured in Chromium on the `picker-select-ru` fixture with a motionless hover, the host was replaced ~600ms in and the explanation simply disappeared. `anchorTooltipKeys` (and the badge's equivalent) now record what each live surface says, so a re-annotate is a no-op while the copy would be identical and rebuilds only when the hidden-language list actually changed. The entry chips get the same treatment — less consequential there, but it stops them repainting and restarting their fade on every pass.

**Roles, not tags.** `<ul>` looks like the obvious signal and is the wrong one: the `picker-survivor-uk` e2e fixture is a `<ul class="lang-switcher">` laid out with `display: flex` — a one-line strip — and reading the tag would have flipped the existing, working shape onto the new path. An explicit `role="listbox"` is a deliberate authoring act that says "stacked menu of options", and it survives a redesign in a way a class or a layout measurement does not. A native `<select>` is forced to `'inline'` even when it claims `role="listbox"`: the browser draws its popup outside the document, so a chip among its `<option>`s would render nowhere.

Restore is unchanged in effect and careful about ordering: the chip's own `detach()` puts back the inline `display` it snapshotted, which is the `none !important` `hideElement` had already written — so `restorePickerInPlace` drops the chips first and un-hides after, and the page-wide "Show everything on this page" sweep already ran in that order (`detachCurtains()` then the `[data-movar-hidden]` pass).

`pickers/bigfive-listbox` is the corpus pin — the first list-shaped fixture in a corpus that was entirely header strips, captured from the live page with its 42 rows intact, since the ratio is the point: the fan-out was eight tooltips scattered down a long list, not eight in a list of nine. Picker manifests can now pin `layout`, and `picker-listbox.behavior.spec.ts` drives the real extension over the shape in Chromium — zero tooltips, one chip in the hidden row, and a hit-test proving every other row is still what the pointer lands on.
